How it works

Three steps, and you never leave your own hard drive.

No upload, no sign-in, no moving files. Track2Mix reads the XML that Rekordbox already exports, decodes the audio on your disk, and writes what it finds to one database file next to it.

Export your collection

In Rekordbox: File → Export Collection in xml format. That file is the only thing Track2Mix needs to find your library.

Let it listen

Every track is decoded — MP3, FLAC, WAV, AIFF, M4A — and measured for loudness, dynamics, brightness, movement and rhythmic density, in parallel across all your cores. A big library takes minutes, not an evening.

Dig, build, blend

Click any track for its ranked, compatible neighbours. Audition them, tick the keepers, let the playlist builder order them, and export an M3U8 that Rekordbox imports directly — or blend them on the Compose timeline.

What it measures

Key and BPM find the candidates. Energy puts them in order.

Genre tags and star ratings describe what you thought about a track the day you filed it. Track2Mix decodes the audio instead, because the only thing that reliably predicts whether two records sit together is what's in the waveform.

  • Loudness and dynamic range. How hard it hits, and how much it breathes. A compressed roller and a track that swings from silence to full weight can average out the same; telling them apart is most of the job.
  • Brightness. Where the weight of the sound sits. It separates dark and sub-heavy from crisp and forward long before you'd call it a genre.
  • Movement. How much the sound changes moment to moment. A track that evolves and a track that loops can share every other number and feel nothing alike in a mix.
  • Rhythmic density. How busy the percussion is, independent of tempo. Two tracks at the same BPM can be miles apart here, and it's usually what makes a transition feel rushed or flat.

Those four combine into one energy score from 0 to 10, weighted so the result matches how a set feels rather than what a meter says. Every score is visible and sortable, so you can check it against tracks you know cold. Keys come from Rekordbox's own analysis, the ones already on your CDJs.

Audition before you commit

Compose

Hear the set before you play it.

A multi-lane timeline for the tracks you picked: one lane per track, a mixer strip on each, and a transport that plays the whole thing in real time. It's a sketchpad for a set, not a replacement for your decks.

  • Auto-arrange lines it up. It finds each track's beat grid and downbeat, trims every clip to start on one, and brings each track in on an 8-bar phrase of the one before it, with a 4 to 32-bar blend. You drag, trim and fade from there.
  • A real mixer on every lane. Volume, three-band EQ with full kills, high- and low-pass filters, mute, solo, and vinyl-style tempo sync to the master BPM.
  • Three ways out, and a way back. Record live, bounce to WAV, or export a 320 kbps MP3 with the encoder built in. Save it all as a .t2m project and pick it up tomorrow.
Everything in the mixer and on the timeline
  • Clips snap to the beat grid; hold Alt to bypass. Drag to move, pull the edges to trim, the corner handles to set fades. Auto-crossfade blends every overlap unless you've drawn the fade yourself.
  • Sync folds a lane to half or double time when that's the closer match. Pitch follows tempo; there is no key-lock yet.
  • Auto-arrange orders by harmonic flow, energy rise or lane order, and corrects a track's BPM when its detected tempo is confident and within 2% of the Rekordbox tag.
  • Record live keeps your fader and EQ moves. Bounce and MP3 export render offline, faster than real time, with ID3 tags on the MP3.
  • A .t2m project holds the master BPM, every lane's mixer, every clip and a snapshot of each track, so it reopens even after Rekordbox renumbers its ids. Unsaved changes are flagged and the app asks before discarding them.
  • Space plays or pauses anywhere. Ctrl/Cmd+O, S and Shift+S handle projects. Delete removes a clip, Ctrl/Cmd+wheel zooms, and a double-click resets any knob.

Scope

What it does, what it doesn't, and what it runs on.

Plenty of tools promise AI that builds your set. Track2Mix doesn't, because that isn't a solved problem. Here is the actual scope, so you can decide in thirty seconds whether it's useful to you.

  • Measures energy from the audio itself, not from genre tags or guesswork.
  • Handles real, messy libraries. Moved drives can be remapped with a path map; streaming stubs and dead entries are reported instead of silently dropped.
  • Does not detect key itself. It uses Rekordbox's keys. A track unanalysed there has no key here either.
  • Does not write back to your collection. It exports playlists. Your collection file and your audio are never modified.
  • Does not pick the set for you. It narrows eight thousand tracks to a handful worth auditioning and will order and beat-match the ones you choose. The choosing is still yours.

Sends

Questions

The things people ask first.

Does this touch my Rekordbox collection?

No. Track2Mix only reads the XML file you export. It writes the playlists you export, its own SQLite database, Compose project files, and any WAV or MP3 you render. Your collection file and your audio files are never modified.

How long does analysis take?

It depends on your machine and your library, and it runs on every core you have. Because Track2Mix samples three 30-second stretches of each track rather than decoding whole files, it is far cheaper than a full Rekordbox re-analysis. The import screen reports how many entries resolved to files on disk before a single file is decoded.

Why does it use Rekordbox's keys instead of detecting its own?

Because you already trust Rekordbox's keys and they are what show up on your CDJs. A second, disagreeing key detector would create confusion rather than value. If a track is unanalysed in Rekordbox, analyse it there first.

Serato, Traktor, Engine DJ?

Not yet. Track2Mix reads the Rekordbox XML format today. The analysis engine is library-agnostic, so other sources are a matter of adding parsers. If you want one, say so when you sign up and it moves up the list.

Does Compose replace my DJ software?

No, and it isn't trying to. Compose is for hearing whether a sequence works: lay the tracks out, let Auto-arrange beat-match and blend them, adjust the EQ and fades, and render the result. Tempo sync is vinyl-style, so pitch follows tempo and there is no key-lock yet. It is a sketchpad for a set, not a performance tool.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

No catch on the money. The full app downloads free with nothing held back, and you tip only if it earns it. A fixed price on an unproven tool would filter out exactly the DJs whose feedback matters most; tips say what people actually value and fund what gets built next. What's free today stays free.

Does my music or data get uploaded anywhere?

No. There is no backend. Analysis runs locally, results are stored locally, and the app makes no network calls. The only things that reach me are the name and email you enter to get the download. They go to my mailing list and nowhere else, and you can leave in one click.
