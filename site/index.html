Three rings, read from the inside out.

Rekordbox can draw the first two. Key narrows eight thousand tracks to the ones that can sit next to the anchor; tempo keeps the ones that can be played with it. The third ring is the one Rekordbox has no reading for.

  1. Key. Same slot on the Camelot wheel, one step either way, or the relative major or minor. Four of twenty-four. 8,412 to 1,402
  2. Tempo. Inside your BPM tolerance, with half and double time counted, so a 174 still finds its 87s. 1,402 to 118
  3. Energy. Measured from the audio: loudness and dynamics, brightness, movement, rhythmic density. It decides the order, and the five worth auditioning. 118 to 5

What the detector reads.

Genre tags and star ratings describe what you thought about a track the day you filed it. Track2Mix decodes the audio instead. Four signals, three 30-second stretches per track, one energy number from 0 to 10 that you can sort by and check against the tracks you know cold.

Keys stay Rekordbox’s own. The app reads them from the export so the results match what is on your CDJs, and it never writes anything back.

From a Rekordbox export to the shortlist.

  1. Export

    In Rekordbox: File, then Export Collection in xml format. That one file is all Track2Mix needs to find your library.

  2. Listen

    Every track is decoded on your own machine, MP3, FLAC, WAV, AIFF or M4A, on every core you have. Minutes for a big library, not an evening. Nothing is uploaded; there is no server.

  3. Dig

    Click the track you’re on. Its compatible neighbours come back ranked. Audition them, tick the keepers, order them in Playlist and export an M3U8 that Rekordbox imports directly.

Then hear the set before you play it.

Compose is a multi-lane timeline for the tracks you picked: one lane per track, a mixer strip on each, and a transport that plays the whole thing in real time. It is a sketchpad for a set, not a replacement for your decks.

  • Auto-arrange finds each track’s beat grid and downbeat and brings it in on an 8-bar phrase of the one before, with a 4 to 32-bar blend.
  • A real mixer on every lane: volume, three-band EQ with full kills, high- and low-pass, mute, solo, vinyl-style sync.
  • Record live, bounce to WAV, or export a 320 kbps MP3. Save it all as a .t2m project and pick it up tomorrow.

Questions.

Does it touch my Rekordbox collection?
No. It reads the XML you export and writes only the playlists you export, its own database, Compose projects and the audio you render. Your collection file and your audio files are never modified.
How long does analysis take?
Minutes, not an evening, on every core you have: three 30-second stretches per track instead of whole files. The import screen says how many entries resolved to files on disk before anything is decoded.
Why Rekordbox’s keys and not its own?
Because you already trust them and they are what shows on your CDJs. A second, disagreeing key detector would add confusion, not value. A track unanalysed in Rekordbox has no key here either.
Serato, Traktor, Engine DJ?
Not yet. The engine is library-agnostic, so other sources are a matter of parsers. Say which one you want when you sign up and it moves up the list.
Does Compose replace my DJ software?
No. It is for hearing whether a sequence works: lay the tracks out, let Auto-arrange line them up, adjust the EQ and fades, render the result. Sync is vinyl-style, so pitch follows tempo; there is no key-lock yet.
Free, so what’s the catch?
None on the money. The full app downloads free and you tip only if it earns it. A fixed price on an unproven tool would filter out exactly the DJs whose feedback matters most. What is free today stays free.
